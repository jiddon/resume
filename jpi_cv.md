## Part B. CV SUMMARY

My research line focuses on the construction, commissioning, validation, and operation of next-generation silicon tracking detectors. Across ALICE ITS2, the ATLAS Pixel detector, ATLAS ITk Pixel and Strip, and Belle II VTX, my work has connected detector design, hardware-software integration, system verification and validation, and long-term operational performance in large-scale collider experiments.

During my PhD at the University of Liverpool and CERN, I contributed to the construction, characterisation, and verification of 25% of the ALICE ITS2 Outer Barrel, one of the largest monolithic pixel detector systems ever built. I developed verification software integrated with production readout and commissioning workflows, defined detector test procedures and subsystem validation criteria, and led the first cosmic-muon studies of the ITS2 Outer Barrel. I also carried out the first full-system detection-efficiency measurement using 5 million cosmic muon tracks.

After the PhD, I developed this research line further through positions of increasing responsibility in ATLAS Pixel detector operations at CERN. I developed operational tools and CI/CD-supported workflows for mission-critical detector operation, coordinated calibration activities, and contributed to DAQ software for continuous LHC operations. As ATLAS Pixel Run Coordinator, I held formal responsibility for detector readiness decisions and coordinated 24/7 operations supported by a 300-person international pool.

In parallel, I contributed to future detector development through work on ATLAS ITk Pixel and Strip and, more recently, Belle II VTX. I am currently applying this experience at IFIC / Universitat de València, where I work on Belle II DMAPS telescope development and silicon tracker integration.

I have published 414 papers, received 7009 citations, and have an h-index of 44. I have also contributed to the supervision and mentoring of early-career researchers, to undergraduate teaching, and to detector operations training.

My long-term goal is to consolidate an independent research line at the interface of detector instrumentation, systems engineering, and operational reliability for frontier particle-physics experiments.
